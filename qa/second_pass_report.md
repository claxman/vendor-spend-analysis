## 1. What each stage looked for

- **The known error first.** `V0116` Orionw LLC was classified as an outsourced IT contractor on the strength of hint 60 after two searches found nothing (the first query carried the hint's framing, "Orionw LLC software development", and the second was the bare name). It is a Singapore technology, media and telecoms law firm. The cause, a hint followed where the search found nothing, applies to every row that was unverified and hinted: 24 rows, all re-verified fresh in Stage 2 (the "same cause" group below). Three of them had the wrong reading: `V0116` (a law firm, not an IT contractor), `V0065` Pingo (a painting and glazing contractor, not an unidentified payee) and `V0358` Platinum Office (a business consultancy, not office supplies). The others were confirmed or stayed unfound.
- **Stage 1, all 386 rows, no web.** Four reviewer agents that had not seen the classification read each row's research finding beside the final department, sub_function, cost_cluster, recommendation and description, with `docs/taxonomy.md`, `docs/clusters.md` and `docs/recommendation_rules.md`, and flagged every row where the final row does not follow from the finding, follows a hint the finding contradicts, or breaks a rule.
- **Stage 2, fresh verification.** Agents that saw neither the hint nor the first-pass row established from the vendor's own site or a reliable directory what the vendor does, in one line with its source, for: every Stage 1 flag; the 24 same-cause rows; every row at or above $10,000; then a seeded random sample of 30 of the remaining rows (seed 2026), in that order, until done or the search limit stopped them. The findings were then compared with the first pass in the main session, and every disagreement resolved: the row changed, or kept with a one-line reason.
