# Top 3 opportunities

The three largest savings in the acquired business's third-party spend, ranked by the base-case annual saving. The figures are from `outputs/analysis/savings_summary.md`, rounded to the nearest $1,000, except that each calculation is written out in whole dollars so that it can be reproduced; the exact model is in `outputs/analysis/savings.md`.

## 1. Exit the offices under a remote operating model, keeping one hub space

The acquired business pays $896,000 a year for offices and coworking from seven providers in six countries (The Office Group, Zagrebtower, IndiQube, GPT Space&Co, WeWork, WorkEZ, Common Desk) and running costs. The model assumes a remote operating model that keeps one hub space in Zagreb, right-sized at the next break, and ends 75% of the $837,043 of leases and coworking ($627,782), applied evenly to every lease row, and 70% of the $59,297 of running costs ($41,508): $669,290 a year, $669,000 rounded ($532,000 to $801,000). Cross-check: closing every site except Zagreb, with its lease untouched, would save $653,000, 78% of the leases, so the 75% sits just below that. Year 1 is $335,000, half a year, as notice dates are unknown. Without the decision, consolidating onto one flexible provider is worth $221,000. Main risk: notice periods and exit costs are unknown.

Estimated annual saving (USD, base case): $669,000

## 2. Move back-office services to the group's shared functions

The acquired business spends $1,750,000 a year on auditors and accountants in six countries (BDO, 4i, Grant Thornton), recruiters, law firms, payroll, insurers and brokers, HR and finance systems (Sage, Planful) and HubSpot. Each line moves to its group function at its own rate: 35% of the $553,641 of audit, tax and accounting ($193,774), 100% of the $149,179 of recruiters ($149,179), 80% of the $101,762 of HR and finance systems ($81,410) and the other twelve lines ($229,093): $653,456 a year, $653,000 rounded ($459,000 to $830,000). Year 1 is $359,000: the auditor changes at year-end and systems save after migration. Without the decisions, tenders and seat reviews are worth $198,000. Main risk: filings and payroll must not lapse in the handover.

Estimated annual saving (USD, base case): $653,000

## 3. Renegotiate the Salesforce platform agreement at change of control

Salesforce UK is $3,117,000, 40% of spend: the product runs on its platform. Such products normally pay Salesforce a partner revenue share, not per-seat licenses. The contract's first question is which applies, and the rate tier. A revenue share moves with revenue, not seat counts, and turns on the rate tier, what counts as net revenue and the company's own seats. Salesforce's published standard share is 15% of net revenue, 25% for embedded apps, and 25% until May 2017. The saving is small on that best rate and large on the older 25%. Licenses would mean a seat true-down and renewal terms. Until the contract shows which reading applies, the base takes the middle of the 5% to 25% range modeled on the platform agreement: 15% off the $3,117,226 agreement ($467,584), 20% off the $208,675 document app ($41,735) and 10% off the $52,825 PSA tool ($5,283): $514,602 a year, $515,000 rounded ($179,000 to $850,000). Year 1 is $257,000 (renewal date unknown). Main risk: the platform dependency, so terms, never exit.

Estimated annual saving (USD, base case): $515,000

Second wave: $1,411,000 of travel, software, cloud, telecom, marketing, contractor and unidentified spend carries a base saving of $392,000 (range $240,000 to $616,000; $258,000 in year 1), mostly vendor action the acquired business can take itself.

Spend that falls away without action: $274,000 of deal and one-off costs end on their own ($227,000 of M&A advisers and the data room, $47,000 of one-off payments) and are not counted above.

People-linked spend that this review does not count: $398,000 of health and life cover, statutory cover, benefit schemes, employer-of-record pay and student labor, less the small savings already counted on those lines; it moves only with the workforce plan.
