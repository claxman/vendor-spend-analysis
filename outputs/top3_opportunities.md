# Top 3 opportunities

The three largest savings in the acquired business's third-party spend, ranked by the base-case annual saving. The figures are from `outputs/analysis/savings_summary.md`, rounded to the nearest $1,000, except that each calculation is written out in whole dollars so that it can be reproduced; the exact model is in `outputs/analysis/savings.md`.

## 1. Exit the offices under a remote operating model, keeping one hub space

The acquired business pays $896,000 a year for offices and coworking from seven providers in six countries (The Office Group, Zagrebtower, IndiQube, GPT Space&Co, WeWork, WorkEZ, Common Desk) and running costs. The model assumes a remote operating model that keeps one touchdown space at the Zagreb engineering hub and ends 75% of the $837,043 of leases and coworking ($627,782) and 70% of the $59,297 of running costs ($41,508): $669,290 a year, $669,000 rounded, in a range of $532,000 to $801,000. Year 1 is $335,000, as notice dates are unknown and half a year is assumed. Without the decision, consolidating onto one flexible provider is worth $221,000. Main risk: notice periods and exit costs are not yet known.

Estimated annual saving (USD, base case): $669,000

## 2. Move back-office services to the group's shared functions

The acquired business spends $1,750,000 a year on auditors and accountants in six countries (BDO, 4i, Grant Thornton), recruiters, law firms, payroll, insurers and brokers, HR and finance systems (Sage, Planful) and HubSpot. Each line moves to its group function at its own rate: 35% of the $553,641 of audit, tax and accounting ($193,774), 100% of the $149,179 of recruiters ($149,179), 80% of the $101,762 of HR and finance systems ($81,410) and the other twelve lines ($229,093): $653,456 a year, $653,000 rounded ($459,000 to $830,000). Year 1 is $359,000: the auditor changes at year-end and systems save after migration. Without the decisions, tenders and seat reviews are worth $198,000. Main risk: filings and payroll must not lapse in the handover.

Estimated annual saving (USD, base case): $653,000

## 3. Renegotiate the Salesforce platform agreement at change of control

Salesforce UK is $3,117,000, 40% of spend, because the product runs on its platform; with add-ons the platform spend is $3,383,000. The product cannot leave the platform: the negotiation is price and terms, never exit. The model assumes, at change of control, co-termed with any Salesforce agreement the group holds and with internal seats trued down separately, 15% off the $3,117,226 platform agreement ($467,584), 20% off the $208,675 document app ($41,735) and 10% off the $52,825 PSA tool ($5,283): $514,602 a year, $515,000 rounded ($335,000 to $850,000). Year 1 is $257,000, as the renewal date is unknown. The figure is the value of a negotiation that starts once the contract and usage data arrive; main risk: the platform dependency.

Estimated annual saving (USD, base case): $515,000

Second wave: $1,411,000 of travel, software, cloud, telecom, marketing, contractor and unidentified spend carries a base saving of $392,000 (range $240,000 to $616,000; $258,000 in year 1), mostly vendor action the acquired business can take itself.

Spend that falls away without action: $274,000 of deal and one-off costs end on their own ($227,000 of M&A advisers and the data room, $47,000 of one-off payments) and are not counted above.
